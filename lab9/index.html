2. 2025 GDP Choropleth

Geographic position and country shape are preserved, while color represents nominal GDP.

Position → Geographic Location Shape → Country Boundary Color → GDP
2025 Nominal GDP (billion USD, logarithmic scale)

A logarithmic color scale is used because GDP is highly skewed. A linear scale would allow the very largest economies to dominate the color range and make many other countries difficult to distinguish.

3. 2025 GDP Dorling Cartogram

Each economy is represented by a circle positioned near its geographic location. Circle area represents nominal GDP.

Position → Approximate Geography Circle Area → GDP Color → GDP
Important: Circle area represents GDP rather than actual geographic land area. Country shape and precise adjacency are intentionally sacrificed.

4. Design and Comparison

The choropleth and cartogram emphasize different aspects of the same GDP data. In the choropleth, the United States and China appear with the strongest colors, followed by economies such as Germany, Japan, and India. However, the geographic area of each country remains unchanged. This preserves recognizable country shapes, locations, and regional relationships, making the choropleth useful for identifying geographic patterns and seeing where high or low GDP values occur.

The Dorling cartogram changes this emphasis by representing each economy with a circle whose area is proportional to GDP. The United States and China become visually dominant, while smaller economies occupy much less space. This makes differences in economic magnitude easier to compare because area directly represents GDP. However, exact country shape, boundary, and adjacency are lost, and positions may shift slightly to reduce overlap. The cartogram therefore better supports comparisons of economic size, while the choropleth better supports geographic interpretation. Changing from color encoding to area encoding makes differences in global economic size more visually prominent, but at the cost of geographic fidelity.
